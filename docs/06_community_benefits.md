# Community benefits model
Status: proposed measurement and governance framework.

## Sources of value
Potential value includes lower comparable electricity costs, reduced effort finding offers, clearer bills, collective negotiating information, and reliable community communication. Future ancillary services may create additional value, but they are not counted as proven MVP benefits. Community coordination alone does not ensure a discount or eligibility.

## Benefit waterfall
For the same account, period, consumption and included charge scope:
1. Counterfactual baseline cost minus actual/quoted alternative cost = gross saving (possibly negative).
2. Subtract any consumer-paid platform/savings fee and applicable switching/exit costs.
3. Subtract an explicitly consented community allocation if one exists.
4. Report retained household savings, then separately show optional upgrade fees and resulting net household benefit.

Report refundable deposits as cash tied up, not permanent expense; describe financing or forfeiture costs separately where relevant. The simple CSV model assumes no deposits or exit costs and uses comparable all-in rates; actual evaluation must add these through a detailed bill comparison before claiming realized value.

Example only: 300 kWh × PHP 10.00 comparable baseline = PHP 3,000; 300 kWh × PHP 9.40 = PHP 2,820; gross saving PHP 180. With a 0% savings fee and 0% community allocation, retained saving is PHP 180. If that member independently buys a PHP 99 upgrade, net cash benefit is PHP 81. These prices are illustrative, not market quotations. If the alternative rate rises to PHP 10.20, gross saving is negative PHP 60 and must be shown as a loss.

## Avoid overstating benefits
Do not apply a generation-only discount to the whole bill. Normalize usage, billing days and charge coverage; distinguish tariff changes from behavior, seasonality and meter changes. Do not add a community transfer to household retained savings and call both new value. Supplier-paid matching fees are platform revenue, not another consumer benefit. Keep estimated annualized savings separate from measured period savings. Disclose non-participants and losses alongside averages.

## Measurement ledger
Maintain pseudonymous account ID; eligibility route/date; consent; baseline and comparison periods; consumption; each material charge; source bill/offer; normalization method; platform fee; upgrade fee; community allocation; net benefit; verification status; reviewer/date. Use medians, totals and participant distribution with counts, not a headline percentage alone. Record effort saved through a time-use survey separately from cash savings.

## Community governance
Members must understand who represents them and how an offer is accepted. Community allocation defaults to zero. Any future fund needs opt-in authority, purpose, custody, approvals, reporting and dispute handling before money is collected. The platform should not hold funds in the MVP. Publish aggregate outcomes only when they cannot reasonably expose individual household usage; define suppression rules in privacy review. A message board supports announcements, questions and feedback, but posts are not consent to a contract or binding votes unless an independently valid process is implemented.

Assign a complaint owner. Allow members to challenge calculations and correct data. Retain evidence of corrections. Treat accessibility, trust, participation and complaint resolution as separate outcomes rather than converting them into invented monetary benefits.
