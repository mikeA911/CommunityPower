# Management go/no-go brief
Recommendation: proceed to bounded feasibility; do not treat this reconstruction as approval to build or launch. All gates below are proposed management criteria.

## Decision and funding sequence
First authorize feasibility with a named sponsor, scope, spending cap, and reviewers. Its cost is not covered by an established estimate here. Next decide whether to release the indicative PHP 400k–700k MVP build envelope. Only then authorize a controlled pilot of 1–3 communities, with a separately funded operating runway. The 8–10 week assumption describes the scoped build after prerequisites, not elapsed time through regulatory review and pilot evidence gathering.

## Gate A: feasibility before full build
| Evidence needed | Proposed owner | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Route and authority memo | Philippine energy counsel/compliance reviewer | Actual meter/account structure, community authority, platform role and applicable current rules documented; no unresolved blocker |
| Community commitment | Pilot lead | At least one named sponsor, recruitment route, consent plan and documented member interviews |
| Supplier participation | Commercial lead | Preferably two credible counterparties willing to assess the same opportunity; if only one, label limited coverage and obtain an explicit exception |
| Offer economics | Analyst | Comparable cost waterfall includes fees, deposits, taxes treatment and downside; no reliance on whole-bill percentage claims |
| Privacy and operations | Product/operations lead | Data responsibilities, access, retention, moderation and escalation assigned |
| Delivery and finance | Sponsor and delivery lead | Written scope and quote, cost exclusions, runway and acceptance tests agreed |

If a legal route is unavailable for household accounts, assess a separately authorized common-area or other eligible account use case. Do not present this as automatic household aggregation. If the route remains unclear, pause commercial activation and continue only research or educational prototyping.

## Gate B: pilot launch
All critical MVP acceptance criteria pass; all actual participants have valid consent; supplier/representative contracts and required authorizations are in place; no critical security defects remain; bill baseline and measurement methods agreed; members understand no guaranteed savings; operational owner and incident procedures ready.

## Gate C: pilot evaluation
Proposed observation window: 8–12 weeks after onboarding, extended if two comparable billing cycles are not available. This is separate from build time. Recruit 1–3 communities but evaluate each separately.

Proposed learning thresholds to agree before recruitment: at least 30% of invited reachable accounts join; at least 80% of submitted bill records become usable after review; all published offers disclose material charges and compensation; at least two comparable post-activation bill periods for measured savings; median measured participant benefit positive after applicable fees, with loss cases investigated; at least 60% of surveyed active users say they would continue, with sample size and response rate shown. These are newly proposed targets, not prior agreements or sufficient statistical proof. Report counts with percentages, especially for small samples.

Track actual eligible fraction, referral-to-activation conversion, opt-out rate, support minutes per active member, moderation workload, fee collection rate, acquisition spend per activation, and willingness to pay. No paid-upgrade or provider-fee assumption should be accepted solely from survey intent.

## Decision outcomes
**Go to a limited next phase:** no critical compliance/privacy issue, measurable net participant value, supplier follow-through and a defensible path to contribution improvement. Sponsor approves scope and runway.
**Revise and repeat:** lawful route and demand exist, but onboarding, value, pricing or cost assumptions miss targets. Record a time-bounded corrective experiment.
**No-go/pause:** unresolved legal route, misleading savings, inability to secure permissioned data, no suitable supplier offer, unacceptable participant harm, or no credible cost/revenue path. Explain how member data and obligations will be closed out.

Decision record: date; approver; community/account scope; evidence links; exceptions; remaining risks; budget released; expiry/review date. None of these gates has been passed by this package.
