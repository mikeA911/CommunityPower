# Community Power concept paper
Status: reconstructed proposal. Preserved anchors are identified in the README.

## Purpose and opportunity
Community Power would help community members understand electricity bills and assess whether coordinated demand can secure worthwhile offers. The hypothesis is that community organization, better data, and transparent comparisons can reduce search effort and improve bargaining outcomes. Both demand and achievable savings need field validation.

The Philippine consumer-choice policy context provides a reason to investigate the opportunity. It does not establish that a homeowners association, condominium, group of households, or any arbitrary collection of meters can switch or aggregate. Eligibility, metering configuration, representative authority, current rules, and supplier willingness must be established for each pilot.

## Initial audience
Proposed initial users are residents or account holders in an organized community, its authorized manager or association, Community Power operations staff, and qualified prospective suppliers. Start with communities that have a named sponsor, an understandable meter/account structure, members willing to share data with consent, and a plausible route to eligibility. Common-area accounts and individually metered households must be assessed separately.

## Value proposition
Consumers receive free basic bill records, understandable comparisons, community updates, and visibility into estimated and measured benefits. Optional paid upgrades may provide richer histories, alerts, and personalized reporting; willingness to pay is untested. Community managers receive an aggregate demand view, a consent-aware participation funnel, and coordination tools. Suppliers may receive qualified, permissioned opportunities, subject to lawful commercial arrangements and documented customer choice.

## Operating model
1. Community sponsor expresses interest and documents authority.
2. Members join voluntarily, consent to a defined data purpose, and submit bills or manual entries.
3. Operations checks data quality and separates account types. Aggregate kWh provides a usage picture; it does not prove coincident kW demand or legal eligibility.
4. A qualified reviewer assesses a lawful route and records unresolved requirements.
5. Suitable suppliers are invited on consistent terms. Offers are normalized for energy charges, remaining charges, taxes, deposits, term, escalation, and exit obligations.
6. Members review options and decide through the applicable authorized process. The platform records interest and referrals; contracting and switching remain with authorized parties.
7. The platform compares subsequent bills using a documented baseline, records complaints, and updates community outcomes.

Community Power is positioned as a supplier-neutral software and coordination service. This positioning does not itself determine licensing obligations. The platform must disclose commercial relationships, sponsored content, comparison coverage, and ranking criteria. Payment by a provider must not silently improve its offer ranking.

## MVP proposition
A responsive web application combines onboarding, bill records, aggregate demand, a manually curated offer comparison, savings dashboard, a bounded AI assistant, and a moderated community message board. Manual operations are acceptable during validation. A free tier remains useful without an upgrade. Paid-tier access can be trialed manually before integrating payments.

## Validation strategy
Conduct feasibility before committing to the full build and before a 1–3 community pilot. Test three linked questions: can the proposed arrangement operate lawfully, do members gain measurable value, and can revenue support delivery costs without compromising neutrality? A pilot is a learning exercise and is not expected to establish mature company profitability.

## Principal risks and responses
Eligibility failure: verify the actual accounts and route before commercial promises. Weak savings: compare complete costs and include a no-switch outcome. Low participation: interview members and simplify consent and entry. Commercial conflicts: disclose fees and separate sponsorship from ranking. Privacy loss: limit access and aggregation visibility. Message-board abuse: define moderation and escalation. AI error: constrain answers to approved content and route consequential questions to humans.

## Decision sought
Approve a bounded feasibility workstream with a sponsor, assigned reviewers, and a separate cost estimate. Release build and pilot funding only after the gates in the go/no-go brief are met. Preserve the PHP 400k–700k and 8–10 week assumptions as targets to test against a scoped delivery quotation.
